Dear AC/SPC, our contribution is a controlled comparison of the 3 ways to place sign compression around Muon’s orthogonalization, and of applying error feedback before versus after it. We prove all plain placements can ascend and establish gradient-side EF convergence.

On federated CNN2/CIFAR-10 (11 IID clients, 5 seeds), SignMuon reaches 85.72% at 1 bit per matrix entry, versus Muon 85.98% and SignSGD 81.44%. On centralized ResNet18/CIFAR-10 (3 seeds), it reaches 94.60% versus Muon 94.35% and SignSGD 93.37%. In response to the reviews, we completed learning-rate tuning for all 10 methods on federated CNN2/CIFAR-10 with 11 heterogeneous Dirichlet(0.1) clients, 2000 rounds and no local updates. Selection uses 5000 held-out validation images and seed 0, with momentum 0.9. Over 5 final seeds, EF21-MuonSign reaches 84.03±0.52% and EF21-MuonUSign 83.84±0.32%, versus Muon 46.02±8.55% and SignMuon 42.96±5.94%. See our response to 7S1b, Q2 for the full table. These results favor SignMuon on IID data and gradient-side error feedback for heterogeneous federated learning.

Based on the reviews, we extended EF21-MuonUSign and EF21-MuonSign convergence to fixed EMA/Nesterov and deterministic learning-rate schedules, and verified the tracking and convergence proofs in Lean. The constant/cosine bounds quantify expected gradient norms under the submitted separable-layer assumption. We separately proved the squared-gradient rate with gradient-dependent steps. Our added SignMuon theorem allows interacting layers under joint smoothness and uniform alignment between signed updates and momentum; measuring that alignment during network training remains open.

We also strengthened the failure results. Composing the linear objectives with softplus makes them smooth and lower bounded while preserving the ascent directions for all 3 plain sign placements. For Tx34, our added EF21-SignMuon examples use convex quadratics with fixed smoothness and initial gap. Orthogonalization amplifies a small oscillating gradient component; the error-feedback estimator then keeps missing a fixed nonzero component. Consequently the gradient norm stays at least 1, including with constant learning rates and fixed EMA/Nesterov chosen for the run length. The construction also covers strictly decreasing rates to zero without momentum and suitable power-decay schedules with fixed momentum, using exact polar updates. Its exact algebraic certificate has been checked.
