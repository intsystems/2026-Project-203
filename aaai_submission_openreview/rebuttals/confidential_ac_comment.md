Dear AC/SPC, our paper explores how to combine Muon with sign compression. We investigate signing before, after or on both sides of orthogonalization and applying error feedback at different stages. The exploration identifies different successful choices: SignMuon leads our centralized vision study, whereas only EF21-MuonUSign and EF21-MuonSign retain over 80% in our heterogeneous federated test. We support these findings with convergence guarantees, conditions for SignMuon convergence and explicit failure constructions.

On centralized ResNet18/CIFAR-10 (3 seeds), SignMuon reaches 94.60% versus Muon 94.35% and SignSGD 93.37%. In response to the reviews, we completed learning-rate tuning for all 10 methods on federated CNN2/CIFAR-10 with 11 Dirichlet(0.1) clients, 2000 rounds and no local updates. Rates use 5000 held-out validation images and seed 0, with momentum 0.9. Over 5 final seeds, EF21-MuonSign reaches 84.03±0.52% and EF21-MuonUSign 83.84±0.32%, versus Muon 46.02±8.55% and SignMuon 42.96±5.94%. See our response to 7S1b, Q2 for the full table.

Based on the reviews, we extended EF21-MuonUSign and EF21-MuonSign convergence to fixed EMA/Nesterov and deterministic learning-rate schedules, and verified the tracking and convergence proofs in Lean. The constant/cosine theorems bound expected gradient norms under the submitted separable-layer assumption. We separately proved the squared-gradient rate with gradient-dependent steps. Our added SignMuon theorem allows interacting layers under joint smoothness and uniform alignment of signed updates with momentum; measuring this condition during network training remains open.

For D4r2, composing the linear objectives with softplus makes them smooth and lower bounded while preserving ascent for all 3 plain sign placements. For Tx34, our new exact-polar EF21-SignMuon examples have a nonzero gradient projection onto a fixed direction in parameter space. Every compressed update is perpendicular to that direction. The parameters never change along it, leaving that gradient projection unchanged and the full gradient norm at least 1. This yields failure with rates decreasing to zero: any strictly decreasing schedule without momentum, and shifted power decay with fixed EMA/Nesterov. Separately, for any budget T and any constant rate and fixed momentum chosen for T, a convex quadratic with the same smoothness and initial gap keeps the gradient norm at least 1 throughout. Thus tuning those fixed parameters to the run length also fails to provide a uniform vanishing rate. The exact algebraic certificate has been checked.
