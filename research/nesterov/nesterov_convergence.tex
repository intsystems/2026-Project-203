\begin{abstract}
We prove convergence for the precise Nesterov filter in the paper's
server-side-LMO algorithm. Under its smoothness assumptions, both uplink-only
and bidirectional EF21 compression attain an expected squared nuclear-gradient
rate $O(T^{-1/2})$, with the sharp step and horizon-dependent momentum.
Under the paper's generalized smoothness and local lower bounds, the exact
downlink variant attains an expected nuclear-gradient rate $O(T^{-1/4})$,
with a constant-within-run radius. All tracking recursions, initialization
terms, and sufficient constants are supplied below. This is a mathematical
proof note. A subsequent, independent Lyapunov proof of both rates is now
kernel checked in \texttt{lean\_proofs/EF21Muon/Nesterov/} and included in
the shared article appendix \texttt{ef21\_nesterov\_convergence.tex}.
That appendix supplies the constants matched to Lean; this earlier note
retains its original constants and is not itself included by the articles.
\end{abstract}

\section{Exact algorithm and assumptions}

Let $f=N^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^N f_j$ on a tuple of $p$ matrix blocks
$X_i\in\mathbb R^{m_i\times n_i}$, where $N,p,m_i,n_i\ge1$ and
$r_i=\min(m_i,n_i)$. Fix $0<\beta\le1$ and put $a=1-\beta$;
the paper's momentum parameter is $\mu=a$. At round $t=1,\ldots,T$,
the algorithm is
\begin{align}
 G_t^{(j)}&=\nabla f_j(W_{t-1})+\varepsilon_t^{(j)},
 &M_t^{(j)}&=aM_{t-1}^{(j)}+\beta G_t^{(j)},\label{eq:momentum}\\
 V_t^{(j)}&=aM_t^{(j)}+\beta G_t^{(j)},
 &g_{t,i}^{(j)}&=g_{t-1,i}^{(j)}+
 C_{D,i}\bigl(V_{t,i}^{(j)}-g_{t-1,i}^{(j)}\bigr),\label{eq:nesterov}\\
 g_{t,i}&=\avg g_{t,i}^{(j)},
 &X_{t,i}&=X_{t-1,i}-\eta_{t,i}\pol(g_{t,i}).\label{eq:step}
\end{align}
The server average is exactly the estimator reconstructed by the paper's
message-aggregation recursion. The downlink is either exact, $W_t=X_t$,
or EF21-P:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:downlink}
 W_{t,i}=W_{t-1,i}+C_{P,i}(X_{t,i}-W_{t-1,i}).
\end{equation}
Initialize $M_0^{(j)}=g_0^{(j)}=g_0=0$ and $W_0=X_0$, with deterministic
$X_0$. We also define the bookkeeping values $V_0^{(j)}=M_{-1}^{(j)}=0$.
These do not introduce a stochastic query before round one.

The oracle is an exact, measurable spectral-ball LMO:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:polar}
 \ip{z}{\pol(z)}=\nuc z,\qquad
 \op{\pol(z)}\le1,\qquad \pol(0)=0.
\end{equation}
Every such selection obeys $\frob{\pol(z)}\le\sqrt{r_i}$ on block $i$.
In particular, the partial polar factor used in the article is allowed.

Write $\mathcal F_{t-1}$ for the history just before the gradients in round
$t$. The query $W_{t-1}$ and all previous state are measurable with respect
to this history. For every worker and block assume
\begin{equation}\label{eq:noise}
 \E[\varepsilon_{t,i}^{(j)}\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]=0,
 \qquad
 \E[\frob{\varepsilon_{t,i}^{(j)}}^2\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]
 \le\sigma_i^2.
\end{equation}
The paper's conditional nuclear-norm variance bound implies this condition.
For the generalized-smooth proof we also use conditional independence of
the workers' noises, as assumed in the article. It gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mean-noise}
 \E[\frob{\bar\varepsilon_{t,i}}^2\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]
 \le\sigma_i^2/N,\qquad
 \bar\varepsilon_{t,i}=\avg\varepsilon_{t,i}^{(j)}.
\end{equation}
Cross-layer independence is unnecessary.

The compressors are conditionally mean-square contractive in the
Frobenius norm:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:contraction}
 \E[\frob{C(z)-z}^2\mid z,\text{prior history}]
 \le(1-\alpha)\frob z^2,\qquad 0<\alpha\le1.
\end{equation}
The parameters are $\alpha_{D,i}$ on the uplink and $\alpha_{P,i}$ on
the compressed downlink. The scaled-sign compressors in the article
satisfy this pathwise with $\alpha_{D,i}=1/(m_i n_i)$ and the same
downlink parameter. Resolving zero ties in either allowed way causes no
change to the proof. In the smooth theorem worker independence is not
needed; \eqref{eq:noise} suffices.

\section{Smooth case: both downlinks}

Assume $f\ge f_*$ and, for all tuples $X,Y$ and every block $i$,
\begin{align}
 \nuc{\nabla_i f(X)-\nabla_i f(Y)}
 &\le L_i\op{X_i-Y_i},\label{eq:smooth}\\
 \nuc{\nabla_i f_j(X)-\nabla_i f_j(Y)}
 &\le L_{i,j}\op{X_i-Y_i}.\label{eq:local-smooth}
\end{align}
All smoothness constants are nonnegative. These are precisely the
article's arbitrary-pair assumptions; their restriction on layer
interactions is retained. Put
\begin{equation}
 \widetilde L_i^2=\avg L_{i,j}^2,
 \qquad F_{0,i}=\avg\frob{\nabla_i f_j(X_0)}^2,
 \qquad \Delta=f(X_0)-f_*.
\end{equation}
Use the sharp schedule $\eta_{t,i}=\gamma_i\nuc{g_{t,i}}$, with fixed
$\gamma_i>0$ within the run. Define
\begin{align}
 \delta_i&=\begin{cases}0,&\text{exact downlink},\\
                         4/\alpha_{P,i}^2,&\text{EF21-P downlink},\end{cases}
 &K_i&=3\widetilde L_i^2(1+2\delta_i),\label{eq:constants1}\\
 A_i(\beta)&=\frac3\beta+\frac{480\beta}{\alpha_{D,i}^2},
 &C_i(\beta)&=3\beta+
       \left(9+\frac{360}{\alpha_{D,i}^2}\right)\beta^2,\\
 B_i(\beta)&=\left(\frac3{\beta^2}+
                   \frac{480}{\alpha_{D,i}^2}\right)K_i+3L_i^2\delta_i.
 \label{eq:constants2}
\end{align}

\begin{theorem}[Smooth finite-time bound and tuning]\label{thm:smooth}
Suppose \eqref{eq:momentum}--\eqref{eq:contraction} and
\eqref{eq:smooth}--\eqref{eq:local-smooth} hold. If, for every $i$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:small}
 2L_i\gamma_i\le1,\qquad
 20r_i^2\gamma_i^2B_i(\beta)\le1,
\end{equation}
then, with $\bargamma=p^{-1}\sum_i\gamma_i$ and
$w_i=\gamma_i/\bargamma$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:smooth-rate-finite}
 \frac1T\sum_{t=1}^T\sum_i w_i
   \E\nuc{\nabla_i f(X_{t-1})}^2
 \le\frac{4\Delta}{\bargamma T}
 +10\sum_iw_i r_i\left(
       \frac{A_i(\beta)F_{0,i}}T+C_i(\beta)\sigma_i^2\right).
\end{equation}
In particular, set
\begin{align}
 Z_i&=\left(3+\frac{480}{\alpha_{D,i}^2}\right)K_i+3L_i^2\delta_i,\\
 c_i&=\left(1+2L_i+\sqrt{20}\,r_i\sqrt{Z_i}\right)^{-1},
 &\beta&=T^{-1/2},\qquad\gamma_i=c_iT^{-1/2}.
 \label{eq:smooth-tuning}
\end{align}
With $\barc=p^{-1}\sum_i c_i$ and $w_i=c_i/\barc$, the left side of
\eqref{eq:smooth-rate-finite} is at most $D/\sqrt T$, where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:smooth-constant}
 D=\frac{4\Delta}{\barc}+10\sum_iw_i r_i
 \left[\left(3+\frac{480}{\alpha_{D,i}^2}\right)F_{0,i}
 +\left(12+\frac{360}{\alpha_{D,i}^2}\right)\sigma_i^2\right].
\end{equation}
A common $c_i$ can instead be chosen below every displayed threshold;
then all weights are one.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Tracking at the actual query}

Fix a block, temporarily suppress its index, and let
$h_t^{(j)}=\nabla_i f_j(W_{t-1})$ for $t\ge1$.
Define $h_0^{(j)}=h_1^{(j)}$ and
\begin{align}
 F_t&=\avg\E\frob{M_t^{(j)}-h_t^{(j)}}^2,
 &D_t&=\avg\E\frob{h_t^{(j)}-h_{t-1}^{(j)}}^2,\\
 I_t&=\avg\E\frob{M_t^{(j)}-M_{t-1}^{(j)}}^2,
 &H_t&=\avg\E\frob{g_t^{(j)}-V_t^{(j)}}^2,\\
 P_t&=\E\frob{W_{t,i}-X_{t,i}}^2,
 &S_t&=\E\frob{X_{t,i}-X_{t-1,i}}^2.
\end{align}
Thus $D_1=0$, $I_0=H_0=P_0=0$, and $F_0$ is the initial constant
already defined. A summation without limits below runs from $1$ to $T$.

\begin{lemma}[EMA error and its increments]\label{lem:ema}
For $0<\beta\le1$,
\begin{align}
 F_t&\le aF_{t-1}+\beta^{-1}D_t+\beta^2\sigma_i^2,\label{eq:F-rec}\\
 \sum_tF_t&\le\frac{F_0}\beta+\frac{\sum_tD_t}{\beta^2}
                    +\beta T\sigma_i^2,\label{eq:F-sum}\\
 \sum_tI_t&\le4\beta F_0+4\sum_tD_t+3\beta^2T\sigma_i^2.
 \label{eq:I-sum}
\end{align}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Condition on $\mathcal F_{t-1}$. Unbiasedness kills the cross term in
\[
 M_t^{(j)}-h_t^{(j)}
 =a\bigl[(M_{t-1}^{(j)}-h_{t-1}^{(j)})
                +(h_{t-1}^{(j)}-h_t^{(j)})\bigr]
        +\beta\varepsilon_t^{(j)}.
\]
For vectors in the Frobenius Hilbert space,
\[
 a^2\frob{u+v}^2
 \le a\frob u^2+(a^2/\beta)\frob v^2
 \le a\frob u^2+\beta^{-1}\frob v^2.
\]
The first inequality also holds at $a=0$ (its two sides are zero).
This proves \eqref{eq:F-rec}. Sum it and discard the nonpositive
terminal term $-aF_T$ to obtain \eqref{eq:F-sum}.
Also, directly from \eqref{eq:momentum},
\[
 I_t\le2\beta^2F_{t-1}+2\beta^2D_t+\beta^2\sigma_i^2.
\]
Use $\sum_tF_{t-1}\le F_0+\sum_tF_t$ and \eqref{eq:F-sum},
then $\beta^2\le\beta$, $\beta^2\le1$, and $\beta^3\le\beta^2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The Nesterov increment and the uplink]\label{lem:uplink}
\begin{align}
 V_t^{(j)}&=(1+a)M_t^{(j)}-aM_{t-1}^{(j)},\label{eq:V-identity}\\
 \sum_t\avg\E\frob{V_t^{(j)}-V_{t-1}^{(j)}}^2
 &\le10\sum_t I_t,\label{eq:V-increment}\\
 \sum_tH_t&\le\frac{160\beta}{\alpha_{D,i}^2}F_0
       +\frac{160}{\alpha_{D,i}^2}\sum_tD_t
       +\frac{120\beta^2}{\alpha_{D,i}^2}T\sigma_i^2.
 \label{eq:H-sum}
\end{align}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Equation \eqref{eq:V-identity} follows by eliminating $\beta G_t$;
it is valid at $t=0$ with the declared bookkeeping values. Consequently
\[
 V_t-V_{t-1}=(1+a)(M_t-M_{t-1})-a(M_{t-1}-M_{t-2}).
\]
The squared norm is at most $8\frob{M_t-M_{t-1}}^2+
2\frob{M_{t-1}-M_{t-2}}^2$. Summation proves
\eqref{eq:V-increment}, since $I_0=0$.

For $0<\alpha\le1$, Young's inequality gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:contracted-sum}
 (1-\alpha)\frob{u+v}^2
 \le(1-\alpha/2)\frob u^2+(2/\alpha)\frob v^2.
\end{equation}
Apply this to $u=V_{t-1}^{(j)}-g_{t-1}^{(j)}$ and
$v=V_t^{(j)}-V_{t-1}^{(j)}$, then use compressor contractivity.
It follows that
\[
 H_t\le(1-\alpha_{D,i}/2)H_{t-1}
       +\frac2{\alpha_{D,i}}\avg\E\frob{V_t^{(j)}-V_{t-1}^{(j)}}^2.
\]
As $H_0=0$, summing yields $\sum H_t\le40\alpha_{D,i}^{-2}\sum I_t$.
Now use Lemma~\ref{lem:ema}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Downlink and query drift]\label{lem:downlink}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:PD-sum}
 \sum_tP_t\le\delta_i\sum_tS_t,\qquad
 \sum_tD_t\le K_i\sum_tS_t.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For an exact downlink $P_t=0$. Otherwise \eqref{eq:contracted-sum}
applied to \eqref{eq:downlink} gives
$P_t\le(1-\alpha_{P,i}/2)P_{t-1}+2S_t/\alpha_{P,i}$.
Summing from $P_0=0$ proves the first inequality.
For $t\ge2$, smoothness and $\op z\le\frob z$ give
\[
 D_t\le3\widetilde L_i^2(S_{t-1}+P_{t-1}+P_{t-2}),
\]
because $W_{t-1}-W_{t-2}$ is the sum of the server displacement and
the two broadcast errors. Sum, use $D_1=P_0=0$, and apply the first
inequality. This proves the second inequality with \eqref{eq:constants1}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Error of the server estimator]\label{lem:server}
Let $e_t=\E\frob{g_{t,i}-\nabla_i f(X_{t-1})}^2$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:server-sum}
 \sum_te_t\le A_i(\beta)F_{0,i}
           +B_i(\beta)\sum_t S_t+C_i(\beta)T\sigma_i^2.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
At a fixed worker, conditional unbiasedness gives the exact identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:local-N-identity}
 \E[\frob{V_t^{(j)}-h_t^{(j)}}^2\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]
 =a^2\E[\frob{M_t^{(j)}-h_t^{(j)}}^2\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]
 +\beta^2(1+2a)
    \E[\frob{\varepsilon_t^{(j)}}^2\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}].
\end{equation}
Indeed the two affine noise coefficients are $\beta(1+a)$ and $\beta$,
and $[\beta(1+a)]^2-a^2\beta^2=\beta^2(1+2a)$.
Jensen's inequality for the worker averages, followed by the squared
three-term triangle inequality, therefore yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:server-rec}
 e_t\le3H_t+3F_t+9\beta^2\sigma_i^2+3L_i^2P_{t-1}.
\end{equation}
The three terms in the decomposition of the error are
\[
 g_t-\bar V_t,\qquad \bar V_t-\nabla_i f(W_{t-1}),\qquad
 \nabla_i f(W_{t-1})-\nabla_i f(X_{t-1}).
\]
All gradient-noise cancellations above are at the predictable query
$W_{t-1}$; none conditions on $X_t$, which depends on the current noise.
Substitute \eqref{eq:F-sum}, \eqref{eq:H-sum}, and
\eqref{eq:PD-sum} in \eqref{eq:server-rec}. The resulting coefficients
are exactly \eqref{eq:constants2}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Descent and closure of the bound}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:smooth}]
For any two block matrices $h,g$, set $b=\nuc g$, $z=\nuc{h-g}$,
and $u=\nuc h\le b+z$. Duality and \eqref{eq:polar} imply
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sharp-pairing}
 \ip{h}{\nuc g\,\pol(g)}\ge b^2-bz
 \ge\tfrac14u^2+\tfrac12b^2-\tfrac52z^2.
\end{equation}
The last inequality follows from $(b-3z)^2/4\ge0$ and $u\le b+z$.
The smooth descent lemma and $L_i\gamma_i\le1/2$ now give
\begin{align}\label{eq:sharp-descent}
 \E f(X_t)\le\E f(X_{t-1})
 &-\frac14\sum_i\gamma_i\E\nuc{\nabla_i f(X_{t-1})}^2
 -\frac14\sum_i\gamma_i\E\nuc{g_{t,i}}^2\notag\\
 &+\frac52\sum_i\gamma_i r_i e_{t,i}.
\end{align}
Here $\nuc z^2\le r_i\frob z^2$, and the upper model follows by
integrating \eqref{eq:smooth} along the actual simultaneous block step.
The Frobenius displacement satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:S-bound}
 S_{t,i}\le r_i\gamma_i^2\E\nuc{g_{t,i}}^2.
\end{equation}
Sum \eqref{eq:sharp-descent} and apply Lemma~\ref{lem:server} and
\eqref{eq:S-bound}. The coefficient of
$\gamma_i\sum_t\E\nuc{g_{t,i}}^2$ on the positive error side is at
most $(5/2)r_i^2\gamma_i^2B_i(\beta)\le1/8$.
Absorb it into the negative coefficient $1/4$ and discard the remaining
nonpositive estimator term. Lower boundedness gives
\[
 \frac14\sum_{t,i}\gamma_i\E\nuc{\nabla_i f(X_{t-1})}^2
 \le\Delta+\frac52\sum_i\gamma_i r_i
           [A_i(\beta)F_{0,i}+C_i(\beta)T\sigma_i^2].
\]
This is \eqref{eq:smooth-rate-finite} after division.
For \eqref{eq:smooth-tuning}, $\beta^2B_i(\beta)\le Z_i$,
so \eqref{eq:small} holds. Finally, for $T\ge1$,
\[
 \frac{A_i(\beta)}T\le
  \frac{3+480/\alpha_{D,i}^2}{\sqrt T},\qquad
 C_i(\beta)\le\frac{12+360/\alpha_{D,i}^2}{\sqrt T}.
\]
These yield \eqref{eq:smooth-constant}.
\end{proof}

\section{Generalized smoothness: exact downlink}

In this section $W_t=X_t$, $\eta_{t,i}=s_i>0$ is constant within a run,
and we replace \eqref{eq:smooth} by the article's condition
\begin{equation}\label{eq:generalized}
 \nuc{\nabla_i g(X)-\nabla_i g(Y)}
 \le\bigl(L_i^{0,g}+L_i^{1,g}\nuc{\nabla_i g(X)}\bigr)\op{X_i-Y_i}
\end{equation}
for every pair $X,Y$ and $g=f,f_j$, with nonnegative constants.
Write $(L_i^0,L_i^1)$ for $f$ and $(L_{i,j}^0,L_{i,j}^1)$ for $f_j$.
Assume $f_j\ge f_{j,*}$, and put
\[
 f_\dagger=\avg f_{j,*},\qquad
 \Delta_0=f(X_0)-f_\dagger,\qquad
 q_i=\sqrt{1-\alpha_{D,i}},\qquad \kappa_i=\frac{q_i}{1-q_i}.
\]
The constants introduced below cover zero generalized-smoothness
coefficients without any division by them.

\subsection{First-moment tracking, including filtered noise}

For the remainder of this subsection fix a block and suppress $i$.
Let $h_t^{(j)}=\nabla_i f_j(X_{t-1})$ and again set $h_0=h_1$.
Define the first-moment quantities
\begin{align*}
 u_t&=\avg\E\frob{M_t^{(j)}-h_t^{(j)}},
 &d_t^{\rm loc}&=\avg\E\frob{h_t^{(j)}-h_{t-1}^{(j)}},\\
 v_t&=\avg\E\frob{g_t^{(j)}-V_t^{(j)}},
 &d_t^{\rm glob}&=\E\frob{\bar h_t-\bar h_{t-1}},
\end{align*}
where $\bar h_t=\nabla_i f(X_{t-1})$. Thus $v_0=0$ and both
drifts vanish at $t=1$. The initial local error is
$u_0=N^{-1}\sum_j\frob{\nabla_i f_j(X_0)}$.

Introduce a noise-only EMA and a de-noised global bias:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:debias}
 Z_0=0,\quad Z_t=aZ_{t-1}+\beta\bar\varepsilon_t,\quad
 B_t=\bar M_t-Z_t-\bar h_t,\quad b_t=\E\frob{B_t}.
\end{equation}
Here $b_0=\frob{\nabla_i f(X_0)}$ and
$B_t=a(B_{t-1}+\bar h_{t-1}-\bar h_t)$ exactly.

\begin{lemma}[First-moment estimator bound]\label{lem:first}
For every positive prefix length $k\le T$, with all sums from $1$ to $k$,
\begin{align}\label{eq:first-error}
 \sum_t\E\frob{g_{t,i}-\nabla_i f(X_{t-1})}
 \le{}&\frac{b_0}\beta+6\kappa_i u_0
       +\frac1\beta\sum_t d_t^{\rm glob}
       +6\kappa_i\sum_td_t^{\rm loc}\notag\\
       &+6\kappa_i\beta k\sigma_i
        +2\sqrt\beta\,k\sigma_i/\sqrt N.
\end{align}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The triangle inequality, conditional Cauchy--Schwarz, and
\eqref{eq:momentum} give
\[
 u_t\le au_{t-1}+a d_t^{\rm loc}+\beta\sigma_i,
 \qquad
 \sum_tu_t\le\frac{u_0}\beta+
        \frac{\sum_td_t^{\rm loc}}\beta+k\sigma_i.
\]
Consequently
\[
 \sum_t\avg\E\frob{M_t^{(j)}-M_{t-1}^{(j)}}
 \le2u_0+2\sum_td_t^{\rm loc}+2\beta k\sigma_i.
\]
By \eqref{eq:V-identity}, the corresponding sum for $V_t-V_{t-1}$
is at most three times this expression. Compressor contractivity and
conditional Jensen give
\[
 v_t\le q_i\left(v_{t-1}+
               \avg\E\frob{V_t^{(j)}-V_{t-1}^{(j)}}\right).
\]
Sum from $v_0=0$ to obtain
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v-sum}
 \sum_tv_t\le6\kappa_i
        \left(u_0+\sum_td_t^{\rm loc}+\beta k\sigma_i\right).
\end{equation}
Next, \eqref{eq:debias} yields
$b_t\le ab_{t-1}+a d_t^{\rm glob}$, whence
\[
 \sum_tb_t\le b_0/\beta+\beta^{-1}\sum_td_t^{\rm glob}.
\]
The martingale difference property and \eqref{eq:mean-noise} yield
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Z-bound}
 \E\frob{Z_t}^2\le\frac{\beta}{2-\beta}\frac{\sigma_i^2}N
 \le\frac{\beta\sigma_i^2}N.
\end{equation}
The effective averaged target satisfies the exact identity
\[
 \bar V_t-\bar h_t=aB_t+aZ_t+\beta\bar\varepsilon_t.
\]
Hence $\E\frob{\bar V_t-\bar h_t}
\le b_t+2\sqrt\beta\,\sigma_i/\sqrt N$, since $\beta\le\sqrt\beta$.
Finally $\E\frob{g_t-\bar h_t}\le v_t+
\E\frob{\bar V_t-\bar h_t}$. Summation and \eqref{eq:v-sum}
prove \eqref{eq:first-error}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Coefficient envelopes from lower boundedness}

Choose
\begin{align}
 M&=1+\max\bigl(\{L_i^1\}_i\cup\{L_{i,j}^1\}_{i,j}\bigr),
 &\Lambda_i&=\max_jL_{i,j}^1,\\
 O&=M^{-1}\sum_iL_i^0,
 &\widetilde O&=M^{-1}\avg\sum_i L_{i,j}^0.
\end{align}
For $\Delta(X)=f(X)-f_\dagger\ge0$, define
\begin{align}
 A_i&=L_i^0+L_i^1O,&B_i&=2ML_i^1,\label{eq:gen-AB}\\
 \widetilde A_i&=\avg L_{i,j}^0+\Lambda_i\widetilde O,
 &\widetilde B_i&=2M\Lambda_i.
\end{align}
These symbols $A_i,B_i$ are used only in this generalized-smooth section.

\begin{lemma}[Coefficient envelopes]\label{lem:envelope}
For every $X$,
\begin{align}
 L_i^0+L_i^1\nuc{\nabla_i f(X)}&\le A_i+B_i\Delta(X),\label{eq:gen-global-env}\\
 \avg\left(L_{i,j}^0+L_{i,j}^1\nuc{\nabla_i f_j(X)}\right)
 &\le\widetilde A_i+\widetilde B_i\Delta(X).
 \label{eq:gen-local-env}
\end{align}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For any $g$ satisfying \eqref{eq:generalized} and $g\ge g_*$, take the
simultaneous block step $Y_i=X_i-M^{-1}\pol(\nabla_i g(X))$.
Integrating \eqref{eq:generalized} along this segment gives
\[
 g(Y)\le g(X)-M^{-1}\sum_i\nuc{\nabla_i g(X)}
       +\frac1{2M^2}\sum_i
          \left(L_i^{0,g}+L_i^{1,g}\nuc{\nabla_i g(X)}\right).
\]
Since $M\ge L_i^{1,g}$ and $g(Y)\ge g_*$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:self-bound}
 \sum_i\nuc{\nabla_i g(X)}
 \le2M(g(X)-g_*)+M^{-1}\sum_iL_i^{0,g}.
\end{equation}
Use $g=f$, $g_*=f_\dagger$ to obtain the first envelope. Apply
\eqref{eq:self-bound} to each $f_j$, average, and use
$N^{-1}\sum_j(f_j(X)-f_{j,*})=\Delta(X)$. Multiplication by
$\Lambda_i$ proves the second envelope.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Finite-time and asymptotic bounds}

Define nonnegative constants
\begin{align}
 U_i&=s_i^2\left(\frac12+\frac{2\sqrt{r_i}}\beta\right),
 &V_i&=12\kappa_i s_i^2\sqrt{r_i},\\
 \nu&=\sum_i\left(12\kappa_i s_i\sqrt{r_i}\,\beta\sigma_i
          +4s_i\sqrt{r_i\beta}\,\sigma_i/\sqrt N\right),\\
 C_0&=\Delta_0+2\sum_i s_i\sqrt{r_i}
                      (b_{0,i}/\beta+6\kappa_i u_{0,i}),\\
 d&=\sum_i(U_iA_i+V_i\widetilde A_i)+\nu,
 &b&=\sum_i(U_iB_i+V_i\widetilde B_i).
 \label{eq:gen-constants}
\end{align}

\begin{theorem}[Generalized-smooth Nesterov convergence]\label{thm:gen}
Suppose \eqref{eq:momentum}--\eqref{eq:contraction},
\eqref{eq:generalized}, and the local lower bounds
hold. Use an exact downlink and a constant radius $\eta_{t,i}=s_i$.
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gen-finite}
 \sum_{t=1}^T\sum_i s_i\E\nuc{\nabla_i f(X_{t-1})}
 \le(1+b)^T(C_0+Td).
\end{equation}
In particular, for any fixed $c_i>0$, choose
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gen-tuning}
 \beta=T^{-1/2},\qquad s_i=c_iT^{-3/4},\qquad
 \barc=p^{-1}\sum_ic_i,\qquad w_i=c_i/\barc.
\end{equation}
There is an explicit constant $D_{\rm gen}$, independent of $T$, such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gen-rate}
 \frac1T\sum_{t=1}^T\sum_iw_i\E\nuc{\nabla_i f(X_{t-1})}
 \le D_{\rm gen}T^{-1/4}.
\end{equation}
In particular the minimum expected weighted gradient norm satisfies
the same bound. No bounded-gradient hypothesis is added.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Put $\Delta_t=\E[f(X_t)-f_\dagger]$. Exact oracle duality gives
\[
 \ip{\nabla_i f(X)}{\pol(g_i)}
 \ge\nuc{\nabla_i f(X)}-2\nuc{g_i-\nabla_i f(X)}.
\]
The generalized descent lemma, $\op{X_{t,i}-X_{t-1,i}}\le s_i$, and
$\nuc z\le\sqrt{r_i}\frob z$ imply
\begin{align}\label{eq:gen-descent}
 \Delta_t+\sum_is_i\E\nuc{\nabla_i f(X_{t-1})}
 \le{}&\Delta_{t-1}
   +2\sum_i s_i\sqrt{r_i}\E\frob{g_{t,i}-\nabla_i f(X_{t-1})}\notag\\
   &+\sum_i\frac{s_i^2}2\E
          [L_i^0+L_i^1\nuc{\nabla_i f(X_{t-1})}].
\end{align}
For $t\ge2$, \eqref{eq:generalized} with the coefficient evaluated at
$X_{t-2}$ gives
\begin{align*}
 d_{t,i}^{\rm glob}&\le s_i\E
       [L_i^0+L_i^1\nuc{\nabla_i f(X_{t-2})}],\\
 d_{t,i}^{\rm loc}&\le s_i\avg\E
       [L_{i,j}^0+L_{i,j}^1\nuc{\nabla_i f_j(X_{t-2})}].
\end{align*}
Both drifts vanish at $t=1$. Thus, for every prefix $k$, their sums are
bounded by the corresponding coefficient sums at $X_0,\ldots,X_{k-1}$,
multiplied by $s_i$. Sum \eqref{eq:gen-descent}, substitute
Lemma~\ref{lem:first}, and then Lemma~\ref{lem:envelope}. This gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:prefix}
 Y_k:=\Delta_k+\sum_{t=1}^k\sum_is_i\E\nuc{\nabla_i f(X_{t-1})}
 \le C_0+kd+b\sum_{t=0}^{k-1}\Delta_t.
\end{equation}
All terms in $Y_k$ are nonnegative, so $\Delta_t\le Y_t$ and
$Y_0=\Delta_0\le C_0$. An elementary induction gives
\[
 Y_k\le(C_0+kd)(1+b)^k.
\]
For completeness, at induction step $k$ bound each earlier $Y_t$ by
$(C_0+kd)(1+b)^t$ and use
$1+b\sum_{t=0}^{k-1}(1+b)^t=(1+b)^k$, an identity also valid at $b=0$.
Drop $\Delta_T\ge0$ to prove \eqref{eq:gen-finite}.

Here are explicit horizon-independent constants for \eqref{eq:gen-rate}:
\begin{align*}
 \bar U_i&=c_i^2(1/2+2\sqrt{r_i}),
 &\bar V_i&=12\kappa_i c_i^2\sqrt{r_i},\\
 \bar C_0&=\Delta_0+2\sum_ic_i\sqrt{r_i}
                    (b_{0,i}+6\kappa_i u_{0,i}),\\
 \bar d&=\sum_i(\bar U_iA_i+\bar V_i\widetilde A_i)
       +\sum_i\left(12\kappa_ic_i\sqrt{r_i}\sigma_i
                    +4c_i\sqrt{r_i}\sigma_i/\sqrt N\right),\\
 \bar b&=\sum_i(\bar U_iB_i+\bar V_i\widetilde B_i),
 &D_{\rm gen}&=e^{\bar b}(\bar C_0+\bar d)/\barc.
\end{align*}
Indeed \eqref{eq:gen-tuning} implies
$C_0\le\bar C_0$, $Td\le\bar d$, and $Tb\le\bar b$ for all $T\ge1$.
In particular $TU_i\le\bar U_i$ and $TV_i\le\bar V_i$;
the two noise powers are $Ts_i\beta=c_iT^{-1/4}$ and
$Ts_i\sqrt\beta=c_i$. Since $(1+b)^T\le e^{Tb}$ and
$T(p^{-1}\sum_i s_i)=\barc T^{1/4}$,
\eqref{eq:gen-finite} gives \eqref{eq:gen-rate}.
\end{proof}

\section{Integrability and scope}

All expectations used above are well-defined and finite. In the smooth
case, global Lipschitz gradients have at most linear growth. Starting
from deterministic finite state, \eqref{eq:noise}, the finite-dimensional
norm comparisons, and conditional compressor contractivity inductively
give square-integrable state at every finite round. Smooth $f$ has at
most quadratic growth, so its values are integrable. In the
generalized-smooth case the normalized steps put every finite-horizon
trajectory in a fixed compact set, independently of the sampled
gradients. Continuity bounds the objectives and gradients there;
\eqref{eq:noise} and contractivity then give the remaining integrability.
The exact partial polar and scaled-sign selections are measurable.

The result concerns the paper's Nesterov \emph{filter}
$V_t=\mu M_t+(1-\mu)G_t$ at the same queried model. It does not change
the query to a classical extrapolated Nesterov point. The algorithm
order, zero initialization, and server aggregation are those of
\texttt{aaai\_article/signmuon\_appendix.tex}, Algorithm
\texttt{alg:fed\_serverlmo}.

Theorems~\ref{thm:smooth} and~\ref{thm:gen} close the Nesterov-filter
extension of the article's two theoretical rates. Their constants are
conservative and are not asserted to match the EMA constants in the
article or in \emph{Error Feedback for Muon and Friends}
(\url{https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.00643}). The arguments above are
self-contained; they do not invoke that paper's convergence theorems.

The scope remains specific:
\begin{itemize}
\item The sharp step is used for the smooth squared-norm bound; the
generalized-smooth bound uses an exact downlink and a constant-within-run
radius. Its stationarity criterion is a first moment, not a second moment.
\item Both vanishing stochastic rates use horizon-dependent
$\mu=1-T^{-1/2}$. The proofs do not establish a vanishing rate for fixed
$\mu=0.95$ with fixed-variance gradients and the experimental schedules.
\item The oracle is exact. A finite Newton--Schulz approximation needs
an additional error analysis.
\item The arbitrary-pair layer assumptions from the article are
preserved. They imply separability across blocks. This note does not
extend the theorems to arbitrarily coupled neural-network layers.
\item The subsequent Lean development verifies the full stochastic algorithm,
including the paper's round order, with the Lyapunov constants in the shared
article appendix. The constants in this earlier note are separate and are
not claimed to be the constants checked by Lean.
\end{itemize}
