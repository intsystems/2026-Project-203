\maketitle

\begin{abstract}\label{sec:abs}
  SignMuon compresses the Muon update to one bit per parameter by signing the oracle's output. It beats SignSGD in practice, yet it can ascend even on a linear function, and signing \emph{before} the oracle does not repair this: we exhibit small explicit instances on which sign-before (MuonUSign) and sign-on-both-sides (MuonSign) ascend as well, so no placement of the sign around the Linear Minimization Oracle (LMO) descends in general. Error feedback does not rescue sign-after either --- applied to the oracle's \emph{output} it fails for every smoothness constant, step size and momentum --- while applied to the \emph{gradient} it works, EF21-MuonUSign and EF21-MuonSign attaining the standard $\mathcal{O}(T^{-1/2})$ rate. Experiment then reverses the ordering: on CIFAR-10 and on the nanoGPT speedrun the strongest compressed method is sign-\emph{after}-the-LMO, precisely the placement we prove divergent.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
Under a limited link, communication rather than computation is the binding cost of distributed training, and compression is the standard remedy \cite{bernstein2018signsgd,bernstein2019signsgdmajority,beznosikov2020biased}. SignSGD is its extreme point --- one bit per coordinate, at slight cost in accuracy --- but what it discards is structure. Muon exploits precisely that structure, orthonormalizing the momentum matrix before stepping \cite{jordan2024muon,bernstein2024old,shah2025practical}, and transmits a \emph{dense} matrix at full precision. We study the three ways of having both: \textbf{SignMuon}, which signs \emph{after} the LMO, $\operatorname{sign}(\operatorname{LMO}(\cdot))$; \textbf{MuonUSign}, which signs \emph{before}, $\operatorname{LMO}(\operatorname{sign}(\cdot))$; and \textbf{MuonSign}, which signs on \emph{both} sides and, like SignMuon, emits a $\pm1$-valued step, so that uplink and downlink alike cost one bit.

None of the three descends in general. Each can \emph{ascend} on a linear objective, the simplest smooth function there is, at every step size and every momentum: SignMuon on a $4\times4$ gradient (Theorem~\ref{th:1}), MuonUSign and MuonSign on a single $5\times5$ one (Theorems~\ref{th:2}--\ref{th:3}), both shapes minimal. Error feedback, the standard repair for a biased compressor, is unavailable for sign-after: applied to the oracle's \emph{output} it fails for every triple $(L,\eta,\mu)$ (Theorem~\ref{th:ef_div}), so no step-size rule built from the smoothness and momentum constants can save it. Applied to the \emph{gradient} it does work; adapting EF21-Muon~\citep{gruntkowska2025error} gives \textbf{EF21-MuonUSign} and \textbf{EF21-MuonSign}, which attain the standard $\mathcal{O}(T^{-1/2})$ rate at a one- and a two-sided one-bit budget.

Experiment then disagrees with theory. On CIFAR-10 with a ResNet-18, on the nanoGPT speedrun, and on federated CIFAR-10 (Appendix~\ref{app:opt:fed}), the best compressed method is sign-after --- exactly the placement we prove unrepairable --- the two provably convergent variants trailing it by several seed spreads.

\paragraph{Related work.} We build on the LMO reading of Muon \citep{bernstein2024old,pethick2025training,riabinin2025gluon} and on the error-feedback framework of \citet{gruntkowska2025error} for bidirectionally compressed Muon. The sign-before and both-sides placements are new, as are the two EF21 methods; SignMuon was proposed concurrently by \citet{mishra2026signmuon}, whose guarantee Appendix~\ref{app:mishra} shows to be one for a different method. Appendix~\ref{app:opt:related} gives the full account.

\section{Problem Statement}\label{sec:proState}

We consider $\min_{\mathbf{X} \in \mathcal{X}} \{f(\mathbf{X}) := \mathbb{E}_{\xi \sim \mathcal{D}} [f(\mathbf{X};\xi)]\}$ with $f(\cdot\,;\xi)$ continuously differentiable and possibly non-convex; the federated form, over $N$ clients, is \eqref{eq:opt:fed} in Appendix~\ref{app:opt:fed}. The model is a tuple of layers $\mathbf{X} = [\mathbf{X}_1,\dots,\mathbf{X}_p]$, $\mathbf{X}_i \in \mathbb{R}^{m_i\times n_i}$, with $\nabla_i$ the gradient block of layer $i$: Gluon's parameter space \citep{riabinin2025gluon} under one geometry, each layer carrying the spectral norm $\|\cdot\|_{2\to2}$, whose dual is the nuclear norm $\|\cdot\|_{*}$, and $\|\nabla f(\mathbf{X})\|_*^2 := \sum_i\|\nabla_i f(\mathbf{X})\|_*^2$. On a block of width one the oracle only normalizes and all three methods coincide with SignSGD, so the results below need $\min(m_i,n_i)\ge2$ (Appendix~\ref{app:vector}).

\begin{assumption}[\textbf{Lower boundedness}]\label{as:1}
$f(\mathbf{X}) \ge f^*$ for all $\mathbf{X}$; where explicitly invoked, each local $f_j \ge f_j^*$ as well.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[\textbf{Layer-wise smoothness}]\label{as:2}
For every layer $i$ and all $\mathbf{X},\mathbf{Y}$, $\|\nabla_i g(\mathbf{X}) - \nabla_i g(\mathbf{Y})\|_{*} \le L_i^{g}\,\|\mathbf{X}_i - \mathbf{Y}_i\|_{2\to 2}$ for $g = f$ (constant $L_i$) and $g = f_j$ (constant $L_{i,j}$). A weaker $(L^0,L^1)$ form, used only in Corollary~\ref{cor:l0l1}, and what the quantifier over arbitrary $\mathbf{X},\mathbf{Y}$ implies, are in Appendix~\ref{app:prelim}.
\end{assumption}

An LMO minimizes the first-order model over the unit ball of a norm, returning the steepest-descent direction in it. Muon is the oracle of the spectral norm: for $\mathbf{M}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}$ with rank-$r$ SVD $\mathbf{U}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}\mathbf{V}^\top$, the LMO direction is $A(\mathbf{M})=-\mathbf{U}\mathbf{V}^\top$, and we write $\operatorname{polar}(\mathbf{M}):=-A(\mathbf{M})$: an orthogonalization, unscaled. The rescalings the reference implementation and the RMS$\to$RMS reading apply are positive per-layer constants, invisible to $\operatorname{sign}(\cdot)$, which we carry in the step size~\eqref{eq:unit_gain_main} and not in the geometry (Appendix~\ref{app:prelim}).

\section{Theory}\label{sec:theory}

\paragraph{Three placements of the sign.} All three build the same momentum estimate $\tilde{\mathbf{M}}_t$ from the stochastic gradients --- an exponential moving average at coefficient $\mu\in[0,1)$, or its Nesterov variant, both written out in Appendix~\ref{app:opt:signa} --- and take the step $\mathbf{X}_t=\mathbf{X}_{t-1}-\eta_t\,\mathbf{s}_t$ at learning rate $\eta_t$, differing only in where the sign acts on $\tilde{\mathbf{M}}_t$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:three_placements}
\mathbf{s}_t=
\begin{cases}
\operatorname{sign}\!\bigl(\operatorname{polar}(\tilde{\mathbf{M}}_t)\bigr) & \text{(SignMuon: after)},\\[2pt]
\operatorname{polar}\!\bigl(\operatorname{sign}(\tilde{\mathbf{M}}_t)\bigr) & \text{(MuonUSign: before)},\\[2pt]
\operatorname{sign}\!\bigl(\operatorname{polar}(\operatorname{sign}(\tilde{\mathbf{M}}_t))\bigr) & \text{(MuonSign: both)}.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
Throughout, $\operatorname{sign}(0)$ is resolved to an independent random $\pm1$, so that the transmitted alphabet is binary. All three then send one bit per matrix parameter up; SignMuon and MuonSign distribute a $\pm1$-valued object and so cost one bit each way, whereas MuonUSign's server-side $\operatorname{polar}(\cdot)$ is dense. The algorithms, and where each oracle runs when the data are federated, are in Appendix~\ref{app:alg}.

\subsection{Divergence of the Three Sign Placements}
The sign step is itself an LMO, steepest descent in $\ell_\infty$ \citep{bernstein2024old}, so signing a Muon direction composes two oracles, each sound alone --- and the composition is an LMO for \emph{no} norm. An oracle for a norm returns $\mathbf{D}=-A(\mathbf{G})$ with $\langle\mathbf{G},\mathbf{D}\rangle=\|\mathbf{G}\|_{\mathrm{dual}}\ge0$; Theorem~\ref{th:1} provides a gradient on which the composition drives that inner product strictly negative. The guarantee is forfeited in the composition, not in either factor.

We refute the descent property on \emph{linear} objectives, the simplest smooth functions:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lin_obj}
f(\mathbf{X})=\langle \mathbf{G},\mathbf{X}\rangle,\qquad \nabla f(\mathbf{X})\equiv \mathbf{G}.
\end{equation}
Gradient descent and full-precision Muon drive $f\to-\infty$ here, so a rule that instead drives $f\to+\infty$ is unambiguously ascending (Remark~\ref{rem:efsm_bdd} restores Assumption~\ref{as:1} without moving any trajectory). The step is then the constant matrix $\mathbf{s}(\mathbf{G})$ whatever the momentum, so momentum affects neither convergence nor divergence (Proposition~\ref{prop:reduction}) and
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lin_increment}
f(\mathbf{X}_{t})-f(\mathbf{X}_{t-1})=-\eta_t\,\bigl\langle \mathbf{G},\ \mathbf{s}(\mathbf{G})\bigr\rangle .
\end{equation}
A single $\mathbf{G}$ with $\langle\mathbf{G},\mathbf{s}(\mathbf{G})\rangle<0$ therefore makes $f$ increase at every step, and such a $\mathbf{G}$ exists for each placement at small size.

\begin{summarythm}{Theorems~\ref{th:1}--\ref{th:3} (summary)}
There exists $\mathbf{G}\in\mathbb{R}^{4\times4}$ with $\langle\mathbf{G},\mathbf{s}(\mathbf{G})\rangle<0$ for SignMuon, and $\mathbf{G}\in\mathbb{R}^{5\times5}$ with $\langle\mathbf{G},\mathbf{s}(\mathbf{G})\rangle<0$ for MuonUSign and for MuonSign simultaneously. On the corresponding objective~\eqref{eq:lin_obj} each method ascends at every iteration, for every $\eta_t>0$, every $\mu\in[0,1)$ and either momentum rule, and $f(\mathbf{X}_t)\to+\infty$ whenever $\sum_t\eta_t=\infty$.
\end{summarythm}

Both instances are explicit and both inner products exact rationals; Appendices~\ref{app:proof_th1}--\ref{app:proof_th3} state the theorems, prove them and bound the two shapes from below, and Figure~\ref{fig:divergence_plot} (left, centre) shows the ascents. Negative results of this kind exist for uncompressed Muon as well \citep{parshakova2026muondoesconvergeconvex}; ours are due to the compressor rather than the geometry.

\subsection{Error Feedback on the Oracle's Output Fails}

The classical remedy for a biased compressor is \emph{error feedback}: keep what the compressor discarded and fold it into the next message \citep{seide20141bit,karimireddy2019efsign}. We work in its EF21 form \citep{richtarik2021ef21}, which stores an estimator and compresses the \emph{difference} to it, so needing no bounded-gradient assumption. Its most direct use keeps the geometry --- the sign \emph{after} the LMO --- and applies error feedback to the oracle's output: \textbf{EF21-SignMuon} (Algorithm~\ref{ef21_signmuon}) tracks an estimate $\mathbf{d}_t^{\mathrm{est}}$ of the polar factor $\mathbf{D}_t=\operatorname{polar}(\tilde{\mathbf{M}}_t)$ by a scaled sign of the residual,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:efsm_est}
\mathbf{d}_t^{\mathrm{est}}=\mathbf{d}_{t-1}^{\mathrm{est}}+\alpha_t\operatorname{sign}\!\bigl(\mathbf{D}_t-\mathbf{d}_{t-1}^{\mathrm{est}}\bigr),
\qquad
\alpha_t=\operatorname{mean}\bigl|\mathbf{D}_t-\mathbf{d}_{t-1}^{\mathrm{est}}\bigr|,
\end{equation}
and steps $\mathbf{X}_t=\mathbf{X}_{t-1}-\eta_t\,\mathbf{d}_t^{\mathrm{est}}$. This does not repair the placement.
% The three divergence theorems are stated in the appendix, next to their
% proofs, but the paper's numbering is fixed by everything that cites it (the
% code, its READMEs, the reproduction guide): Theorems 1-3 are the three sign
% placements, 4 is this one, 5 is the convergence result. The counter is set by
% hand here and reset in the appendix to keep those numbers.
\setcounter{theorem}{3}
\begin{theorem}[Divergence of EF21-SignMuon]\label{th:ef_div}
For every $L>0$, step size $\eta>0$, momentum coefficient $\mu\in[0,1)$, and either momentum variant, there is an $L$-smooth (Assumption~\ref{as:2}), bounded-below (Assumption~\ref{as:1}) $f:\mathbb{R}^{2\times2}\!\to\mathbb{R}$ on which EF21-SignMuon started at $\mathbf{X}_0=\mathbf{0}$ diverges: for an explicit $c=c(f)>0$, $f(\mathbf{X}_{t+2})-f(\mathbf{X}_t)=c\,L\eta^2>0$ for $t\ge3$, so $f(\mathbf{X}_t)\to+\infty$. No step-size rule $\eta=\eta(L,\mu)$ using only the smoothness and momentum constants can make the method convergent.
\end{theorem}
The construction (Appendix~\ref{app:proof_th4}) turns the shared magnitude against the method: a $2\times2$ target whose large off-diagonal reverses sign every step holds $\alpha_t$ at $\Theta(1)$, which the small diagonal constant then overshoots, and the diagonal estimate settles into a period-two cycle whose average has the \emph{wrong} sign. Neither dimension nor momentum is implicated: $2\times2$ is where the scaled sign's worst-case contraction $1/d$ is most favourable.

\subsection{Error Feedback on the Gradient Works}
The shared magnitude is not itself the defect. What fails is the target: error feedback needs one that varies with the step size, and $\operatorname{polar}(\tilde{\mathbf{M}}_t)$ is not Lipschitz in its argument, so it can move by $\Theta(1)$ between consecutive rounds however small $\eta$ is. Compressing the \emph{gradient} in its place restores that dependence, and the mechanism is then EF21-Muon~\citep{gruntkowska2025error}. In both directions we take the scaled sign $\mathcal{C}(\mathbf{Y}) = \operatorname{mean}|\mathbf{Y}|\,\operatorname{sign}(\mathbf{Y})$: one bit per parameter, plus one scalar per layer. \textbf{EF21-MuonUSign} takes the pair $(\mathcal{C},I)$ --- compressed uplink, full-precision model back --- maintaining a gradient estimator $\mathbf{g}_t^{\mathrm{est}}$ from the residual $\tilde{\mathbf{M}}_t-\mathbf{g}_{t-1}^{\mathrm{est}}$ exactly as in \eqref{eq:efsm_est}, and taking the step $\mathbf{X}_t=\mathbf{X}_{t-1}-\eta_t\mathbf{D}_t$ with $\mathbf{D}_t=-A(\mathbf{g}_{t}^{\mathrm{est}})$, the polar factor of the estimator rather than of $\nabla f$. \textbf{EF21-MuonSign} takes $(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{C})$, one bit each way, its downlink a second error-feedback loop on the \emph{model}: a server model $\mathbf{X}_t$ takes the exact LMO step and never leaves the server, and a broadcast model $\mathbf{W}_t$ receives its one-bit update and is the only model the rest of the method observes, while the guarantee bounds the gradient at $\mathbf{X}_t$ (Appendix~\ref{app:opt:fed}).

Both descend where the placements they repair ascend: Figure~\ref{fig:divergence_plot} (centre) has them on the $5\times5$ instance. Appendix~\ref{app:hyp} shows them to be \emph{exact instances} of the EF21-Muon framework, the one substantive check being that the scaled sign is contractive (Lemma~\ref{lem:signcontr}), so they inherit its guarantees: under Assumptions~\ref{as:1}--\ref{as:2} both reach $\min_{t\le T}\mathbb{E}\|\nabla f(\mathbf{X}_t)\|_*^2=\mathcal{O}(T^{-1/2})$, the rate uncompressed Muon attains --- one bit changes the constant, not the rate. The second loop costs a step size smaller by $\sqrt{r}$ in the layer rank, unavoidably, the scaled sign contracting in the Euclidean and not in the layer norm (Remark~\ref{rem:normmismatch}).

\section{Experiments}\label{sec:exp}


We compare all six sign-based methods against Muon, SignSGD, SGD and Adam. Only the base rate $\eta_0$ is tuned: layer $\ell$, of shape $m\times n$, gets $\eta_\ell=\eta_0\lambda_\ell$ with
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:unit_gain_main}
\lambda_\ell=\sqrt{m}\,/\,\|\mathbf{P}_\ell\|_F
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\lambda_\ell^{\textsc{lmo}}=\sqrt{\max(1,m/n)},
\qquad
\lambda_\ell^{\textsc{sign}}=1/\sqrt{n},
\end{equation}
$\mathbf{P}_\ell$ being the matrix the method applies, of exactly known Frobenius norm in either family. The criterion is that every layer's update have the same root-mean-square input--output gain \citep{yang2023spectral,large2024modular}; its \textsc{lmo} branch reproduces the aspect-ratio factor the reference Muon implementation applies \citep{jordan2024muon}, which licenses the same criterion for the \textsc{sign} family, where no convention exists (Appendix~\ref{app:lrscale}). On a deterministic convex quadratic the alignment $\rho_t=\langle\nabla f,\mathbf{D}_t\rangle/(\|\nabla f\|_F\|\mathbf{D}_t\|_F)$ is measurable directly: Theorems~\ref{th:1}--\ref{th:3} give instances with $\rho_t<0$, but on random ones all three placements keep $\rho_t$ away from zero (Appendix~\ref{app:images_task}), so the counterexamples describe a worst case and not a typical one --- which is what permits the results below to run contrary to them.

We train a ResNet-18 adapted to low-resolution images on CIFAR-10 \cite{krizhevsky2009cifar} for $75$ epochs, $\eta_0$ cosine-annealed to zero and selected per method on a held-out split. Numbers average three seeds; differences below the seed spread are not claimed (Appendix~\ref{app:repro}).

SignMuon ranks first, but by less than one seed spread over Muon (Figure~\ref{fig:cifar_results}; numbers in Table~\ref{tab:cifar_main}, Appendix~\ref{app:opt:cen}): what that supports is that it \emph{matches} Muon at one bit per parameter, not that it exceeds it. What does resolve is the $1.2$ points over SignSGD, which spends the same budget without the \textsc{lmo}; the geometry accounts for it, not the compressor. Error feedback is not free here, EF21-SignMuon lying $0.29$ points below SignMuon against standard deviations of $0.11$ and $0.15$. Both families order the placements after, before, both sides. Federation sharpens this: across $N=11$ clients the three span $2.8$ points in the same order, and SignMuon exceeds SignSGD by $4.3$ at one bit in \emph{both} directions (Appendix~\ref{app:opt:fed}).

\subsection{Language Modelling}\label{sec:exp_nanogpt}

\begin{figure}[!tb]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.50\textwidth]{images/nanogpt/fig_nanogpt_main.pdf}
\caption{NanoGPT speedrun, $8\times$H100: validation loss against optimizer step, one run per method; dotted is the target $3.28$, and EF21-MuonSign is drawn at its broadcast model $\mathbf{W}$. The inset magnifies the boxed tail, where SignSGD lies above the range shown.}
\label{fig:nanogpt}

We test where the matrices are large enough for the layer-rank dependence of Corollary~\ref{cor:smooth} to take effect: the modded-nanoGPT speedrun (record \#40), a $12$-layer transformer with $768\times3072$ hidden matrices, on $611$M FineWeb tokens and $8\times$H100 \citep{jordan2024moddednanogpt}. Each method replaces the record's Muon on the hidden matrices and gates, at one $\eta_0$ per \emph{family} fixed by \eqref{eq:unit_gain_main}, so every contrast is matched-hyperparameter (Appendix~\ref{app:nanogpt}).

Three conclusions follow from Figure~\ref{fig:nanogpt} and Table~\ref{tab:nanogpt} (Appendix~\ref{app:opt:nanogpt}). First, the composition transfers to language modelling: all six such methods improve on SignSGD by at least $0.08$ in validation loss, and the best two, EF21-SignMuon and SignMuon, lie within $0.01$ of full-precision Muon at equal wall-clock. Second, on the sign-after placement error feedback is free: those two differ by $0.002$, below the noise level, the target they compress being an orthogonal factor whose entries stay evenly spread. Third, EF21-MuonSign's two models separate at this width. The run has a single client, so its broadcast model $\mathbf{W}$ is the only point at which a gradient is evaluated, and there the compressed downlink costs nothing: $\mathbf{W}$ is indistinguishable from EF21-MuonUSign. The server model $\mathbf{X}$, which the guarantee bounds, settles $2.2$ nats above it --- the mechanism of Remark~\ref{rem:dimension}, not a tuning failure, localized in Appendix~\ref{app:nanogpt} to the zero-initialized MLP output projections.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

A sign step and a spectral step are each an LMO, sound alone; composed, they are an LMO for no norm, and every placement of the sign around the oracle can turn the update into an ascent direction on a linear objective, at every step size and momentum. Where error feedback is applied decides whether it repairs this: on the \emph{gradient} it does, at the standard nonconvex rate; on the oracle's \emph{output}, for no $(L,\eta,\mu)$. All three settings rank the methods in the opposite order. What the counterexamples preclude is a guarantee for every problem in the class, not one holding under conditions ordinary problems satisfy --- identifying those is the open question.
